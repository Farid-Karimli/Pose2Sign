DiT vs. UNet: Evaluating Control Injection Methods for Fine-Grained Motion Synthesis

Abstract
Video generation models can synthesize realistic scenes and human motion from images and text, but they often lack precise control over fine-grained details. Conditional video generation methods address this by grounding the synthesis on structured inputs such as sketches or pose sequences, enabling models to follow specific motion trajectories. However, existing approaches primarily focus on coarse body movement and struggle to reconstruct subtle hand and facial motions. We evaluate two pose-conditioned video generation models on sign language and find that while both replicate the overall arm movement, they fail to preserve accurate finger articulation and interaction. To improve fine-grained fidelity, we isolate the conditioning mechanism and study how pose feature propogate through different architectures. Our results show that distributing conditioning features deeper into DiT blocks improves the reconstruction of detailed hand and facial motion.
